The Scout

Erik's early bets on who's next — wherever they're from, logged as he finds them. Here's the fuller story on each one, not just the two-line version on the homepage.

On the radar

Canyon Static

Fort Collins, CO · Added Jun 2026

Fuzzy, driving guitar pop that sounds like it was recorded in someone's garage on purpose — hooks buried just far enough under the distortion that you have to lean in. Erik caught them at The Aggie and went back a second time a few months later just to see if the songs held up outside a sweaty room full of people singing along. They did. This is the kind of local band that makes you want to check the show calendar every week just in case they're on it again.

Not on streaming yet — the best way to hear them right now is live.
On the radar

Marigold Hour

Loveland, CO · Added Aug 2026

Warm, sad-eyed folk harmonies — the kind of opener that quietly steals the whole night from whoever's headlining. Erik first heard them doing exactly that: a support slot where half the room clearly came in not knowing who they were and left asking. There's a patience to the songwriting that's rare in a live opening act, like they're not in a hurry to win you over because they already know the songs will do it for them.

Not on streaming yet — the best way to hear them right now is live.
On the radar

Rustbelt Radio

Greeley, CO · Added Sep 2026

Alt-country with teeth — reminds Erik of early Drive-By Truckers, the kind of band that writes about a town by writing about the people stuck loving it anyway. Twangy, a little ragged around the edges, and better loud. Newest name on this list, so consider this an early flag rather than a settled opinion — worth catching now while the room is still small.

Not on streaming yet — the best way to hear them right now is live.
On the radar

Ibrahim Maalouf

Beirut → Paris · Added Sep 2026

Not local, and not like anything else on this list — which is exactly why he's here. Maalouf was born in Beirut and grew up in Paris after his family left Lebanon, and he plays a four-valve trumpet built by his father Nassim specifically to hit the quarter-tones that a standard trumpet can't reach — the notes that live between the notes in Arabic classical music. He's spent a career running that instrument through jazz, electronic production, and orchestral arrangements without any of it feeling like a gimmick. If you only know trumpet from jazz standards, this will rearrange your idea of what the instrument can do.
