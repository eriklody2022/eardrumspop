On the radar

Ibrahim Maalouf

Beirut → Paris · Added Sep 2026

Not local, and not like anything else on this list — which is exactly why he's here. Maalouf was born in Beirut and grew up in Paris after his family left Lebanon, and he plays a four-valve trumpet built by his father Nassim specifically to hit the quarter-tones that a standard trumpet can't reach — the notes that live between the notes in Arabic classical music. He's spent a career running that instrument through jazz, electronic production, and orchestral arrangements without any of it feeling like a gimmick. If you only know trumpet from jazz standards, this will rearrange your idea of what the instrument can do.

On the radar

Flamingosis

New Jersey · Added Sep 2026

Aaron Velasquez started making beats in a high school technology class, looping samples in GarageBand before moving to Ableton, which he's used ever since. The stage name comes from his own father's signature move — a one-legged spinning frisbee catch called "the flamingosis" that won him a string of freestyle championships back in the late 1970s. Flamingosis flips rare funk, soul, and soundtrack samples into warm, groove-forward instrumentals — mellow bass, soul-inflected melody, J Dilla and Madlib somewhere in the blood — and was one of the artists who helped kick off the YouTube lo-fi wave. Eight albums in, he's still finding new corners of the crate to dig through.

On the radar

Elder Island

Bristol, England · Added Sep 2026

Katy Sargent, Luke Thornton, and David Havard started out in 2014 as an experimental audiovisual project called Trouvaille, playing folk music before drifting toward house and indie-electronica — a genre swap this deliberate is rare, and it shows in how naturally the two sides coexist now. Sargent's cello and vocals give the songs a warm, organic center that Thornton and Havard build out with synths, programming, and low-end bass. Their self-titled 2014 EP picked up airplay on BBC Radio 6 before Bristol ever noticed; two studio albums and a growing live reputation followed, most recently 2026's Hello Baby Okay.
